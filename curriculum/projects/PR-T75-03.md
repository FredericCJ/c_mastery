# PR-T75-03: Hashed timer-wheel collisions, cancellation and expiry work

## Contract

Implement the four-bucket/four-slot wheel with delay1..16, absolute due ticks, intrusive index links and owner/generation identities. Tick advances one unit and returns every due slot as a bitmask; cancellation unlinks immediately. Extend to a two-level wheel that cascades coarse timers into fine buckets, preserving exact expiry and cancellation identity. Reopen the PR-T50-05 sealed scheduler decision and add this as a third measured alternative to its existing structures under the same timer contract.

The full required exercise is {ref}`X-T75-003`; its shared contracts, D1–D4, objective-specific rubric and delayed variant are part of this project. Eight hours is descriptive accounting, not a ceiling on required work.

## Inputs and expected results

At now0 a delay5 timer maps to bucket1 but must not fire at tick1; it fires exactly at5. Cancelling generation1 then reusing that slot at generation2 makes the old ID `STALE`. Simultaneous due timers all appear in the returned mask, without invoking callbacks during linkage traversal.

## Steps

- **T75.03:** Implement bounded schedule/cancel/tick and a two-level cascade extension with no lost or duplicate expiries, explicit horizon, late tick and immediate timer policies. Complete every exercise deliverable, including separately specified extensions.

## Failure cases

The important wheel tradeoff is not merely replacing a comparison with modulo. Collisions, cancellation frequency, finite identity and concentrated expiries determine correctness and latency. Use an independent sorted map of absolute due times, not another wheel. Exhaust pairs of due1..16 and every cancellation point; verify back-links after each step. Add a delayed-clock jump that crosses three revolutions and either process all missed ticks or specify a bounded late-fire policy. Record the selected scheduler decision before measurement; historical paper VAX numbers are source observations only.

## Hints and recovery

A bucket is a candidate set, not an expiry proof. Cancellation needs both identity validation and neighbor repair. Returning k expiries has at least O(k) observable work.

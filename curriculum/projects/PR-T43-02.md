## Contract

Reuse the failure-preserving [dynamic array](PR-T14-02.md). Add a ledger for twelve successful appends starting empty and doubling capacity when full; unit costs are one insertion plus one copy per existing element. This milestone implements the certificate and observes the existing callbacks, not a new allocator or container. The supplied hosted harness may provide genuine allocated storage; a declared unsigned-char array is not typeless storage for arbitrary typed lvalue access in C17. Alignment alone does not settle effective type.

Use 4 hours implementation and 2 verification. Keep failed acquisition outside the successful-sequence bound and retain the existing unchanged-state acceptance check for failure.

## Inputs and expected results

[The fixture](../../fixtures/performance/PR-T43-02.json) costs 1,2,3,1,5,1,1,1,9,1,1,1: 27 total units, 15 copies and 12 insertions. Charge 3 per append; the final credit is 36−27=9 and every prefix credit is nonnegative. With Φ=2·live−capacity, Φ₀=0 and Φ₁₂=8, the sum of amortized costs is 27+8=35≤36. The first amortized charge is 2; subsequent ones are 3.

At growth from capacity 8 to 16, coexistence requires 24 four-byte slots, or 96 bytes. Final capacity is 64 bytes and live payload 48 bytes. This transient space is separate from a claim about allocator internals.

## Steps

1. Annotate each accepted append with before/after live count, capacity, copies and acquisition outcome. Compare with the twelve literal fixture rows.
2. Compute running actual cost, credit 3k−Σcᵢ and potential after every prefix. Check Σĉᵢ=Σcᵢ+Φₖ−Φ₀, not merely the final total.
3. Inject the prior allocation-failure case; show unchanged pointer/count/capacity and distinguish it from the successful trace.
4. Repeat the worksheet from a declared nonempty initial state. Retain Φ₀ in the telescoping equation; an arbitrary initial state cannot silently borrow the empty-table proof.

## Failure cases

Reject inconsistent count/capacity snapshots and arithmetic overflow. Negative credit disproves the proposed charging scheme under that initial state. A long single growth operation remains expensive even when the sequence average is bounded. Shrink, concurrent mutation, allocator latency and destruction are outside this expansion-only model and must not inherit its guarantee.

## Acceptance and evidence

For .a provide the exact sequence, largest actual cost 9 and total 27. For .b submit all prefix credit/potential rows and the initial-state variant. For .c state that this is a deterministic worst-case sequence guarantee under unit-copy assumptions, not an expectation over random arrivals. Attach actual prior-table callback/count traces and the preserved failure case. The independent oracle verifies these finite rows and transient payload capacity, not the implementation's allocator time or total process memory.

## Hints and recovery

When a doubling append changes live m to m+1 and capacity m to 2m, the potential change pays for copying m elements. Treat the very first allocation separately. If your potential row differs from the fixture, inspect whether you recorded the state before rather than after insertion. Count old and new capacity concurrently during acquisition.

## Transfer challenge

Compare the same accepted table with a fixed-increment growth policy on paper. Derive a sequence-cost counterexample before considering a policy change. Do not charge the container implementation a second time in this module.

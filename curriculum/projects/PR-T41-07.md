## Contract

Implement timers and bounded sending around the established TCP state. Initial/minimum RTO is 1000 ms, maximum cap 60000 ms, estimates follow RFC6298 update order and ambiguous retransmitted RTT samples are ignored. Retransmit earliest unacknowledged segment, double RTO after timeout and abort after three retransmissions under the original resource policy. SYN timeout makes post-handshake initial data RTO at least 3000 ms.

Keep one unacknowledged segment no larger than effective MSS and usable peer window, with conservative fixed cwnd 1 MSS. Retain immutable TCP bytes after DMA completes. Local RX window follows actual storage and reopens only as parser consumption frees space. Zero-window probes begin at current RTO and back off without duplicating or consuming another application byte.

## Inputs and expected results

The fixture samples RTT 80 then 120 ms: SRTT 80/85, RTTVAR 40/40 and clamped RTO 1000 both. A marked retransmitted sample is ignored. Timeout trace sends again at 1000, 3000, 7000 ms then aborts at 15000 ms; RTO cap and SYN-loss floor are separately checked. Flow input distinguishes peer window zero, outstanding flight and opening 256 window. A wrapped flight with SND.UNA 0xfffffff0, SND.NXT 0x10 and window 40 has 32 outstanding octets and 8 usable; it permits no new data under this one-outstanding-segment policy.

Outputs include estimator values, timer origins/deadlines, emitted/retransmitted byte intervals, probe events, ACK-freed storage, usable window and abort cleanup. The oracle checks finite mathematics and events, not production network behavior.

## Steps

1. First 4 h: derive first/second RTT calculations and implement estimator with checked clock/duration arithmetic. Update RTTVAR using the old SRTT.

2. Next 4 h: implement timer start/stop/restart and three-retransmission cleanup. Inject ACK just before/at deadline and never accept a retransmitted RTT sample.

3. Final 4 h: hold peer window zero and local parser consumption, verify probes, reopen update and exact pending bytes. Exercise stale window update guard from the previous milestone.

4. On the isolated cable, use a declared independent peer tool to withhold ACKs/advertise zero then reopen; record captures and actual elapsed times.

5. Compare your policy against the selected congestion passage and list absent growth/fast-recovery/general-event behavior.

## Failure cases

Queueing BUSY without actual send does not start RTO. Invalid ACK frees nothing. Duplicate/old window update cannot release a blocked sender. No new data exceeds peer window/cwnd or becomes a second outstanding flight. Ambiguous RTT and clock wrap/overflow are rejected/handled by the declared clock interface. Abort releases owned buffers and reports failure, not graceful delivery.

## Acceptance and evidence

Retain independent weighted-mean/backoff calculations, every timer transition, zero-window/probe/reopen bytes and actual peer capture if available. The objectives require admissible samples/backoff, capacity/window/no-loss behavior and explicit conservative/full-host comparison. Show DMA completion precedes but does not substitute for valid ACK. A throughput benchmark alone cannot establish these invariants.

## Hints and recovery

If RTO differs on the second sample, update variance before SRTT and apply the 1 s minimum afterward. If probes advance application offset, separate probe sequence handling from new-data consumption. If retransmission data changes, audit immutable retained bytes and their owner.

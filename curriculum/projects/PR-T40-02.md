## Contract

Reuse the bounded byte-channel work from T38.09 and UART completion from T38.02.c. Adapt it to the declared THVD1400 3.3-V boards and add EDU7-v1's two-segment master-granted schedule. This milestone's new implementation is medium-access scheduling and routed bench integration; do not charge the reused channel implementation again.

Each segment is at most 1 m with termination at both ends and a common reference. RE is low; DE is separately controlled. Assert DE 200 us before data, release only after final TC, then allow a peer after a 200 us guard. Local echo is filtered by transmit ownership. Master link 1 starts a turn each 25 ms: at most one DATA followed by zero-payload POLL; slave link 2 replies once with DATA/EMPTY, starting within 2 ms after complete POLL+guard and completing within 10 ms of its first octet.

## Inputs and expected results

The fixture derives ten bits per 8N1 octet, the rounded-up occupancy of 96 octets and legal DE/peer-start events. Its deliberately premature TXE release must fail. Start and completion deadlines are distinct: an on-time reply may legally continue after the start deadline. Host timestamps are injected integer microseconds, never claimed measured waveforms.

Expected outputs include grant state, start/completion timeout counters, echo-filtered peer octets and a waveform index for both actual segments. A missing bench closes software tests only.

## Steps

1. Record board/pins, actual adapter schematic, supply and differential polarity. Check end-to-end termination with power off and explain the approximately 60 ohm parallel result.

2. Reuse the byte/error channel and add per-segment master/slave turn states and finite deadlines. Keep router UARTs independent.

3. Test maximum frame duration, legal EMPTY, no reply, late first byte and legal long reply with virtual time.

4. Measure DE, UART TX and bus differential signal. Correlate TC with the final stop bit; show no driver overlap.

5. Run both segments, then remove/reconnect one cable. Record UART faults/discarded candidates and scheduler recovery without inventing clean bytes.

## Failure cases

An ungranted slave transmission is a counted protocol fault; no overlapping drive is deliberately required to prove rejection. Premature TC assumptions, late start, over 10 ms reply, UART framing/overrun faults and link disconnect produce distinct outcomes. Do not connect an undocumented voltage-domain adapter or substitute RS-232 signals for this differential bus.

## Acceptance and evidence

Retain wiring/termination/part table, two DE/TX/bus captures with settings and timing uncertainty, exact host grant logs, and disconnect/reconnect evidence. The timing oracle checks schedule arithmetic; real measurements close voltage/wiring, final-stop-bit and driver-ownership claims. Demonstrate the three objectives through measured topology, scheduler/TC behavior and an explicit software-versus-physical evidence matrix.

## Hints and recovery

If replies disappear, first compare link grant and DE ownership, then UART errors, then framing; application code cannot repair truncated physical bytes. If a long legal reply is cut off, separate its start deadline from completion deadline. Measure polarity rather than inferring it from a decoder's label.

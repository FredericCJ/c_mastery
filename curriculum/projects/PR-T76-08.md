# PR-T76-08: Field tags, minimal varints and canonical bytes

## Contract

Implement RPMessage with up to8 unique fields, tags1..15 and uint32 values. Wire keys are(field<<3)|0 and all values use minimal base128 uint32 varints. Fields1 and2 are required; field1 is an ID and field2 is a ZigZag-encoded signed delta; unknown advisory3..15 fields are retained semantically. Reject duplicates, other wire types, critical tags>=16, overlong/nonminimal/truncated/overflow varints, missing required fields and excessive field count. Decode accepts any field order; encode sorts tags, preserves unknown values and publishes bytes/length only on success. This is a stated teaching subset, not general Protocol Buffers compatibility.

The full required exercise {ref}`X-T76-008`, its shared contracts and objective rubric are part of this project. Eight hours is descriptive author accounting, not a learner-time or scope ceiling.

## Inputs and expected results

Bytes18 07 10 01 08 96 01 (hex) decode and canonicalize to08 96 01 10 01 18 07. 08 80 00 10 01 is rejected as nonminimal. ZigZag(-1)=1, INT32_MIN maps to UINT32_MAX, INT32_MAX maps to UINT32_MAX-1.

## Steps

- **T76.08:** For five-byte uint32 varints require the final payload<=15 and no continuation. Derive ZigZag using unsigned conversion/shift and a sign mask; decode odd values as -(value>>1)-1 to include INT32_MIN safely. Prove canonical decode/reencode idempotence and uniqueness for the admitted semantic map. Explain the tradeoff of rejecting duplicate fields compared with protocols that merge repeated occurrences. Submit the exercise D1–D4 and retain prerequisite interfaces.

## Failure cases

Run replicated.wire and an independent Python parser/encoder for boundary values and reordered field maps. Kill overlong-varint acceptance and incorrect negative ZigZag mutants. Enumerate every truncation, duplicate field, forbidden tag/wire type and output-capacity boundary; demonstrate two noncanonical orders producing the same canonical bytes.

## Acceptance and evidence

- **T76.08:** Distinguish a wire grammar from one canonical encoding and show why deterministic serialization under one binary is not a universal content identity. Derive portable signed ZigZag arithmetic without signed-left-shift assumptions. Every leaf has a separate rubric criterion in X-T76-008. Public test passage alone does not assess independent understanding.

## Hints and recovery

Separate key varint validation from field-number admission. Then Avoid left shifting a negative signed integer. Record the failed hypothesis before using further hints.

## Transfer challenge

Add length-delimited nested records with depth and total-byte budgets, a critical/advisory negotiation rule and an incremental io.frame decoder. Compare schema migration that retains unknown fields against field-wise reconstruction that loses them. Define canonical NaN/negative-zero/string normalization policy before adding floating or textual values; do not assume the current integer proof extends.

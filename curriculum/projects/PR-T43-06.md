## Contract

Wrap one already accepted [effects component](PR-T13-01.md) and the existing [file adapter](PR-T23-02.md) with a same-clock event ledger. Add a small bounded POSIX-AIO comparison where supported, not a new asynchronous framework: one request/control block/buffer, acceptance, status observation and one result collection. On an unsupported backend, retain the attempted-operation evidence and analyze the supplied synthetic ledger without claiming actual AIO execution.

Use 5 hours implementation and 3 verification. The borrowed request and buffer stay valid while pending; do not access/reuse the buffer until the completed operation and caller-side control flow make release safe. The core semantic comparison is required even when native AIO is unavailable.

## Inputs and expected results

[The fixture](../../fixtures/performance/PR-T43-06.json) uses microseconds on one clock. Blocking return/completion at 10 gives 10 latency. Async submission at 0, return at 2 and completion at 12 gives call span 2 and latency 12. Phases [0,2],[1,11],[10,12] sum to 14 while their union is 12. Two threads consume 8+7=15 CPU units in a 12-unit elapsed window; wall-minus-process-CPU is −3, not a waiting-time estimate.

The third case completes at 1 before submission returns at 2. It is valid. The safe project release boundary is max(return,completion)=2. Throughput uses the declared observation interval and completed count, not the submission-call span.

## Steps

1. Draw submit/return/completion/observation-end events before choosing clock calls. Store clock identity and unit with raw timestamps.
2. Record elapsed plus process/thread CPU observations for the reused component. Separate inclusive serial spans and their nonoverlapping exclusive parts; for concurrent phases calculate union/overlap rather than subtracting overlapping work blindly.
3. For the bounded AIO route, distinguish aio_read acceptance, EINPROGRESS from aio_error and one aio_return after completion. Show request/buffer lifetime and the immediate-completion possibility.
4. Derive latency and throughput for all fixtures. Explain why neither wall-minus-CPU nor a sum of inclusive phases identifies resource waiting.

## Failure cases

Reject timestamp reversal within a declared clock, repeated result collection or early buffer reuse. Do not require completion to occur after return for an asynchronous call. EOF or a failed completion is a semantic result, not automatically a successful completed item in the throughput numerator. Missing events remain missing; do not manufacture exclusive attribution.

## Acceptance and evidence

For .a supply both event ledgers, state/lifetime transitions and the chosen native/unavailable route. For .b supply latency/throughput, serial exclusive attribution and the overlapping union example. For .c supply process/thread/elapsed observations and explain the 15>12 case. Actual tool output proves only the observed operation. The finite oracle certifies the supplied event arithmetic; no complete asynchronous cancellation/liveness proof is claimed.

## Hints and recovery

Label acceptance and final operation result separately in your log format. A completion notification can race ahead of caller return. For exclusive serial time subtract nested completed child spans; for different concurrent threads retain separate spans and union occupancy, because subtraction can produce a misleading result.

## Security

Compaction slides live cells together to close holes, so every name for a cell must follow it. If one alias is left behind it silently names whatever cell moved into the old position, which is a wrong-object read in the modelled heap rather than a crash. The attacker chooses which slots are live, how many handles alias one slot, which payload fields name handles, whether a raw borrow is outstanding when compaction starts and, in the extension, retired or reused handles. The target is your own `or_compact` and `struct or_compact` in `labs/object_runtime`, with 16 synthetic cells and a forwarding array that is kept apart from the heap; it cannot forge valid raw pointers.

Why it matters here. The contract (`labs/object_runtime/CONTRACTS.md`, compaction section) requires every handle to be updated through the forwarding map, payload edges to keep naming handle identities rather than slots, and compaction to refuse with `OR_STATE`, heap unchanged, while a borrow is outstanding, because a borrowed raw address would dangle after the move.

What to try. Take the anchor heap (live slots 2 and 4, handles 0 and 1 aliasing slot 2, handle 2 naming slot 4) and predict the live mask, the handle values, the forwarding entries and the payload positions after compaction. Run `ctest -R object_runtime.compact`; its `compact` function pins `OR_STATE` with the heap byte-identical while the borrow is held, then after release `OR_OK`, live mask 3, handles 0 and 1 both holding 1, handle 2 holding 2, `f[1] == 1`, `f[3] == 2`, payloads 22 and 44 in cells 0 and 1 and the payload edge still holding 3. Then build two variants of your own, one that compacts despite a borrow and one that rewrites only the first handle that names a moved cell, and note which CHECKs fail. The pinned-object and generation-reuse cases in the extensions are tests you add.

What to capture and how to read it. Keep the `OR_CHECK:<line>:<expression>` text, the handle table and payloads before and after, and the heap snapshot compared across the refused call. A second alias still naming the old slot, or a payload edge that now names a slot instead of a handle, is the demonstration. Record commands and source hashes. The assessor's mutation set also checks this invariant.

Impact class. `demonstrated-invariant-violation`, only against a variant you build: a handle resolving to another object's synthetic payload. No secret or authority is crossed, and nothing crashes or hits undefined behaviour.

Remediation and regression. Update every handle through the forwarding map, refuse compaction under a borrow and keep payload edges as handle identities. Keep the alias layouts that exposed your variants as regressions.

Limitations. Raw-pointer use after a move is analysed, not executed; pinning and generation policy are bounded contracts here, and nothing shows a real allocator, cross-process authority or concurrent borrowers.

# Shut down every worker

## Prerequisites

X-T49-003 supply the process, namespace or transport foundations for this investigation.

## Environment

Use the exported linux_supervision workspace and restore the pinned Linux guest with `python3 guest.py setup`. Start with the `shutdown` case in CONTRACTS.md and the fixture interfaces in PROTOCOL.md.

## Problem

One worker is waiting for input. Another may exit before reporting readiness. A later exec may fail after an earlier worker has started. Predict who owns each pipe end and wait obligation at each transition.

## Necessity

Maintain a correct ownership ledger through successful and partially failed startup. Prove bounded shutdown, descriptor closure and terminal status for every owned child. These observations give a reviewer enough information to distinguish different mechanisms that can yield the same visible outcome.

## Reasoning

EOF requires every write end to close. A successful fork gives the parent a wait obligation even if exec fails. Keep exit status, signal termination, readiness and exec outcome separate. Record which worker needed escalation and why waiting for the first successful spawn still matters after the next spawn fails.

## Deliverables

- D1. Record your prediction in `answers/shutdown.json`. Implement supervise_shutdown in src/shutdown.c. Use readiness handshakes and monotonic deadlines for the normal exit, early exit, failed exec with a live peer, and noncooperating worker fixtures. Close every unused pipe end promptly. Record the exit or termination signal, the errno reported across the failed exec, and the final descriptor and child counts.
- D2. Preserve your source, guest serial transcript and run identity. Write an explanation keyed to the measured fields in both the program and the independent outer observations.
- D3. With an EOF reader blocked, identify the single extra reference that would keep the read open. Explain how the deadline and terminal status let a reviewer distinguish that symptom from an early exit.

## Verification

Use `python3 submission.py observe shutdown --program build/learn/supervise --out build/first-shutdown` after building. Run the standard `cmastery check` command in CONTRACTS.md for a fresh graded execution. Explain any discrepancy with your original prediction.

## Rubric

- R1. Maintain a correct ownership ledger through successful and partially failed startup.
- R2. Prove bounded shutdown, descriptor closure and terminal status for every owned child.

## Hints

With an EOF reader blocked, identify the single extra reference that would keep the read open. Explain how the deadline and terminal status let a reviewer distinguish that symptom from an early exit. Use the fixture barriers and monotonic deadlines to order observations; a sleep chosen by trial and error cannot establish readiness.

## Variants

V1 is an individually assigned changed condition after at least seven days. Record the prediction and the observations that would distinguish the alternatives before rerunning the program.

## Security

Use the course’s diskless, networkless guest. The fixture owns namespace creation, controller settings and teardown. The supervisor handles the bounded learner workload as an ordinary guest user.

## Validation

The live CTest checks the selected case against current guest and executable identities. Offline replay verifies trace structure and invariants. A reviewer grades the explanation and changed-condition transfer.

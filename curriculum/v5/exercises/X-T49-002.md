# One process, two PIDs

## Prerequisites

U-T49-08-03, U-T49-09-04 supply the process, namespace or transport foundations for this investigation.

## Environment

Use the exported linux_supervision workspace and restore the pinned Linux guest with `python3 guest.py setup`. Start with the `namespace` case in CONTRACTS.md and the fixture interfaces in PROTOCOL.md.

## Problem

Before boot, draw one process tree from the outer guest and one from the fresh PID namespace. Which kernel objects and policy values will the two views share?

## Necessity

Connect the outer PID to the same process observed as PID 1 inside. Trace the private mount, cgroup membership and task limit from both observers. These observations give a reviewer enough information to distinguish different mechanisms that can yield the same visible outcome.

## Reasoning

The PID printed by a process is meaningful relative to its namespace. A private mount table changes which object a path resolves to. Follow the same outer child through its namespace inode identities. A path string or a changed numeric PID on its own cannot establish that the resource limit changed.

## Deliverables

- D1. Record your prediction in `answers/namespace.json`. Implement supervise_namespace in src/namespace.c. Collect the namespace-init PID, its /proc/self identity and its PID and mount namespace inode numbers. Fork one worker, receive its PID and PPID through a pipe and wait for it. Read the mount marker, cgroup membership and task limit. Join your record to the fixture’s observations of the stopped child before it runs and the leaf after it exits.
- D2. Preserve your source, guest serial transcript and run identity. Write an explanation keyed to the measured fields in both the program and the independent outer observations.
- D3. What evidence would change if the two readers shared the mount namespace? Cite two independent comparisons in your trace.

## Verification

Use `python3 submission.py observe namespace --program build/learn/supervise --out build/first-namespace` after building. Run the standard `cmastery check` command in CONTRACTS.md for a fresh graded execution. Explain any discrepancy with your original prediction.

## Rubric

- R1. Connect the outer PID to the same process observed as PID 1 inside.
- R2. Trace the private mount, cgroup membership and task limit from both observers.

## Hints

What evidence would change if the two readers shared the mount namespace? Cite two independent comparisons in your trace. Use the fixture barriers and monotonic deadlines to order observations; a sleep chosen by trial and error cannot establish readiness.

## Variants

V1 is an individually assigned changed condition after at least seven days. Record the prediction and the observations that would distinguish the alternatives before rerunning the program.

## Security

Use the course’s diskless, networkless guest. The fixture owns namespace creation, controller settings and teardown. The supervisor handles the bounded learner workload as an ordinary guest user.

## Validation

The live CTest checks the selected case against current guest and executable identities. Offline replay verifies trace structure and invariants. A reviewer grades the explanation and changed-condition transfer.

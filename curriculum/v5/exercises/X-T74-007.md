## Security

A generational collector avoids scanning the whole old space by remembering which old objects may point at young ones. A card that should be dirty but is clean is a false negative: a young object reachable only from old space is dropped by the next minor collection. False positives merely cost work. The attacker chooses which old objects store young references, which fields are overwritten or cleared, which objects share a card and when a scan runs. The target is your own `or_card_store` and `or_card_scan` (plus the nursery adapter) in `labs/object_runtime`; it does not write during a scan.

Why it matters here. The anchor puts two objects in one two-object card. Overwriting one young edge must not clean the card while the other young edge is still there, because clearing is only safe after a rescan has found no young edge in any object of that card. The contract (`labs/object_runtime/CONTRACTS.md`, cards section) therefore marks on every young store and clears only after rescanning.

What to try. Predict the dirty mask and the roots bitmap after each step of the anchor: store young 5 from object 3 field 1, store young 7 from object 2 field 0, scan, overwrite the first edge with old target 9, scan, clear the second edge, scan, scan again. Run `ctest -R object_runtime.cards`; its `cards` function pins `dirty == 2` after the first store, roots 80 with four field visits and the card still dirty, roots 64 after the overwrite with the card still dirty, roots 0 and `dirty == 0` after both edges are gone, and zero visits on the next scan. Then build two variants of your own: one where a young store does not dirty its card, and one where an overwrite cleans the card although another young edge shares it. Compare survivors against a full-old-scan nursery, as the Verification section asks.

What to capture and how to read it. Keep the `OR_CHECK:<line>:<expression>` text, the dirty mask and roots bitmap after each step and the four-visits-per-dirty-card count. A young identity missing from the scanned roots while an old edge still names it is the demonstration. Record commands and source hashes. The assessor's mutation set also checks this invariant.

Impact class. `demonstrated-invariant-violation`, only against a variant you build: a live young target missing from the roots in the modelled heap. Nothing is reclaimed or reused, and nothing crashes or hits undefined behaviour.

Remediation and regression. Mark the card on every young store, never clear it on an overwrite, and clear it only after a rescan finds no young edge. Keep the sequences that exposed your variants as regressions.

Limitations. Extra dirty cards are safe and are not attacked. Concurrent writers, real write-barrier code and objects with more than two fields are outside the demonstration.

## Security

**Why this matters here.** A retry schedule is an amplifier: every failed call can turn into several more, and retries from every layer multiply. Three rules keep it bounded here: a retry is allowed only for a request asserted idempotent, no retry may start at or after the shared deadline, and every dispatch, original or retry, spends one token from the common limiter. The jitter has its own trap. Reducing a random draw into a window with a plain modulo favours some values whenever the window does not divide the range, so the draw is reduced by rejection sampling and the rejected remainder is reported as pending, not guessed.

**What to try.** Start from the numbers in the tests. A non-idempotent request is denied before anything is scheduled. With base 2, cap 8 and draws 2, 4, 3 the starts are 2, 6 and 9, and the fourth retry is refused with RZ_FULL even when its draw is 0 (tests/tests.c, retry). A start at tick 9 against deadline 10 is refused with RZ_EXPIRED and the attempt count stays at one. For the transport schedule, take a 4-bit drop mask and predict the sequence: one original and three retries, retry.dispatched==4, the bucket at zero tokens, and a fifth plan refused with RZ_FULL (retry_test.c:222-231). For the reduction, take a span of 3 over an 8-bit sample: 256 % 3 = 1, so sample 0 is rejected and every bin receives 85.

**Evidence to capture.** Build two broken variants, stated as properties: one that skips the limiter charge and one that reduces a draw by modulo. Predict which RZ_CHECK line in resilience.retry_transport rejects each, run it and keep the stderr line. Record the retry count and the token balance your implementation reaches for all sixteen drop masks, and compare it with your hand-derived table. The oracle-style modes print rows and compare nothing, so they help only after you have a model to hold them against.

**How to read the result.** A rejected variant is extra or out-of-window synthetic dispatch, found through well-defined C; it is not a measured load on a real service. The generator makes no entropy claim and its seed record carries explicit entropy limitations, a timeout does not tell you whether the earlier request committed, and retry storms across layers are covered only at the one layer modelled. Modulo bias is a statistical property and not a compromise. The assessor's mutation set also checks this invariant, but that set is not part of what you receive.

Only your own implementation and the synthetic data of this lab are attacked; nothing leaves the process and no external service, hardware or host setting is touched.

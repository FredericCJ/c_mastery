# T64: Numerical contracts and SIMD from first principles

## Purpose and outcomes

Derive numeric representations, rounding, exact and approximate accumulation, scaling and vector tails. Assess accuracy, bounds, cost and changed compiler assumptions separately. Numeric equality is a contract, not a tolerance selected after observing a result.

## Entry check and remediation

Derive integer ranges and floating-point representation error; distinguish overflow, rounding, cancellation and conditioning. Demonstrate a checked array extent and a reproducible timing harness before optimizing.

## Study sequence and teaching

### T64.01 — Fixed-point scale, rounding and saturation

Fixed-point multiplication changes scale twice: the product has twice as many fractional bits, and rounding precedes a checked narrowing. Saturating after a signed overflow is not a repair.

Derive the product scale and prove int32_t multiplication and negation bounds before implementing absolute value. Separate saturation policy from the rounding rule.

Misconception and counterexample: The mutant truncates toward zero, failing positive and negative half-way cases. A signed right shift is not a portable replacement for the specified rounding rule.

Investigation: Implement ns_q15(a,b) for signed Q1.15 operands: multiply exactly in int32_t, round the scaled result to nearest with ties away from zero, then saturate into [-32768,32767]. In particular -32768 times -32768 saturates to32767. Derive the range of every intermediate and distinguish quantization error from saturation.

Evidence: Change to Q3.12 and compare wrap, saturate and error-return APIs. Derive a dot-product accumulator width from maximum length instead of copying the Q1.15 constants.

### T64.02 — Exact rounding model and ties to even

A small rational model separates a rounding rule from the current floating-point environment. It provides independently computable half-way cases for checking target conversions and changed rounding modes.

Derive nearest-neighbor intervals and parity at exact ties. Prove remainder doubling is avoided by comparing r with denominator-r, and explain the negative sign symmetry.

Misconception and counterexample: The mutant rounds every tie away from zero. A test corpus containing only odd lower endpoints misses the defect; a changed rounding mode cannot be assumed to affect every compiler-folded expression.

Investigation: Implement ns_round_even(numerator,denominator,out) for numerator in[-1000000,1000000] and denominator in[1,1000000]. Return the nearest integer, breaking exact ties toward an even integer; leave out unchanged on invalid input. Use integer quotient/remainder, with no floating conversion or rounding library.

Evidence: Compare the rational oracle with volatile double operations under each available fenv rounding mode, retaining compiler flags and FENV_ACCESS support limits. Distinguish conversion behavior from arithmetic rounding.

### T64.03 — Exact accumulation bounds

A product can fit its type while a sum of many products overflows. An exact integer reference is valuable for validating vector rearrangement because addition order then does not alter the specified result.

Derive the maximum positive and negative product and n-times-product bound. Explain which intermediate width is required before multiplication and before addition.

Misconception and counterexample: The mutant narrows the completed sum to32 bits. On a target with a different conversion behavior, report implementation-defined narrowing separately from the violated mathematical output contract.

Investigation: Implement ns_dot(a,b,n,out) for int16_t arrays and0<=n<=65535. Accumulate in int64_t and publish the exact sum; n==0 returns0 without reading either array. Reject n>65535 without changing out. No vector or BLAS library is permitted.

Evidence: Vectorize into several wide partial sums and compare exact results for every tail length. Change n beyond the proved bound and design checked partial accumulation without silently weakening exactness.

### T64.04 — Compensated reduction and cancellation

Small terms can disappear when added to a much larger partial sum. Neumaier compensation retains an estimate of that lost contribution, but depends on a particular arithmetic model and does not solve ill-conditioned data in general.

Derive the correction for each magnitude ordering and identify every rounding point. Compare naive, compensated and exact-rational reference results without claiming a universal relative-error bound near a zero sum.

Misconception and counterexample: The mutant drops compensation and returns0 for [1e16,1,-1e16]. Reassociation permitted by fast-math can invalidate the correction identity even when the source text is unchanged.

Investigation: Implement ns_sum(a,n,out) using Neumaier compensated summation in the supplied order. Accept n<=65535 and finite inputs; reject any nonfinite intermediate or final result without modifying out. Empty input returns0. Assume binary64 and ordinary non-reassociated operations, and record these assumptions explicitly.

Evidence: Implement pairwise summation and a fixed reduction tree for parallel execution. Compare accuracy, stack use, reproducibility and throughput under an equivalent exceptional-value contract.

### T64.05 — Scaled norm and intermediate overflow

The mathematical norm may be representable even when naive squared components overflow. Scaling changes intermediate magnitudes while preserving the desired mathematical result.

State the scale/sumsq invariant in real arithmetic and discuss floating error separately. Prove why the ratio is bounded and identify final overflow as distinct from avoidable intermediate overflow.

Misconception and counterexample: The naive mutant squares3e200 before scaling and rejects a representable5e200 result. Testing only unit-scale data fails to distinguish the two algorithms.

Investigation: Implement ns_norm(a,n,out) for at most65535 finite doubles using scaled sum of squares: retain scale and sumsq so intermediate squares are of ratios<=1. Empty/all-zero vectors return0. Reject nonfinite input or unrepresentable final norm without publishing output. fabs and sqrt are permitted primitives.

Evidence: Handle subnormal inputs under gradual underflow and a documented flush-to-zero target. Compare results and generated instructions, then revise the admitted accuracy contract.

### T64.06 — Matrix layout, bounds and transactional output

Blocking and layout changes are valid optimizations only when indexing, arithmetic and aliasing contracts remain equivalent. A fixed small exact domain provides an independent oracle before timing larger implementations.

Derive the three indexing expressions and accumulator bound; prove rejection is transactional. Count arithmetic and memory traffic under the stated row-major traversal before proposing a cache model.

Misconception and counterexample: The mutant reuses column0 for every result column. Square or identity-only examples can hide layout errors; use rectangular and asymmetric values.

Investigation: Implement ns_matmul(A,B,rows,inner,cols,C) for row-major int32_t arrays with each dimension1..8 and every input element in[-1024,1024]. Produce int64_t C. Reject invalid dimensions or elements before any C write; all arrays are live and disjoint. No matrix library is permitted.

Evidence: Add transposed-B and blocked variants with uneven dimensions and partial tiles. Compare exact output, code size, working set and timing, including cache-warm and cache-cold runs.

### T64.07 — Saturating lanes and scalar tails

A SIMD vector is a collection of fixed-width lanes governed by compiler and instruction-set architecture rules. Scalar promotions, overflow behavior, alignment and tail handling cannot be assumed to carry over unchanged.

Specify lane width, widening, alignment and disjointness. Prove the vector body and scalar tail partition [0,n), and distinguish GCC vector operators from ISO C scalar promotions.

Misconception and counterexample: The mutant rounds n down to a four-lane boundary and leaves the tail unchanged. A benchmark using only multiples of the lane count can conceal this correctness failure.

Investigation: Implement ns_add(a,b,n,out) saturating int16_t sums for n<=65535 with disjoint arrays. Widen each addition, clamp then narrow; handle every element including tails. Empty input succeeds without dereferencing arrays, invalid length fails before writes. Implement an explicit-vector adapter separately and compare it against this scalar oracle.

Evidence: Change lane width and input alignment; implement a runtime-dispatched path with a scalar fallback. Inspect instruction-set and operating-system feature checks, emitted instructions and equal-contract performance distributions.

### T64.08 — Accuracy admission and exceptional values

A numerical comparison needs a declared error model before results are measured. Relative error alone fails near zero; an absolute threshold alone does not scale, and NaNs must not accidentally pass a negated comparison.

Derive the asymmetric expected-relative policy, near-zero behavior and exceptional-value handling. Explain rounding at the acceptance boundary and why a tolerance must come from a prior requirement or error analysis.

Misconception and counterexample: The mutant removes the absolute component, rejecting a permitted near-zero error. Conversely a loose tolerance selected after seeing failures can hide a wrong algorithm.

Investigation: Implement ns_close(actual,expected,atol,rtol) for finite values and finite tolerances with0<=atol and0<=rtol<=1. Use the mathematical target |actual-expected|<=atol+rtol*|expected| and define actual admission by the supplied rounded scaled comparison (boundary rounding is part of this contract), scaling to avoid overflow in difference or threshold. NaN and infinities are rejected, including equal infinities. This is an admission predicate, not proof of an algorithm error bound.

Evidence: Construct condition-sensitive and metamorphic tests whose predictions do not use the implementation output as the oracle. Compare ulp-based and absolute/relative policies around subnormals, signed zero and an ill-conditioned problem.

## Environment and limits

Hosted C17 with int16_t, int64_t and binary64 double verified by the experiment. fabs, fmax, sqrt and isfinite are permitted primitives; no reduction, matrix or SIMD library may implement the assigned mechanism. Explicit GCC vectors are a separately named compiler extension. Required extension evidence includes scalar versus explicit-vector differential tests, emitted assembly and target feature checks, fast-math counterexamples, reproducible resource/timing experiments and changed-size/data-layout transfer. Portable core tests alone do not establish SIMD execution or a performance advantage. New hours are descriptive additions; accepted historical hours and receipts are immutable and do not limit study depth.

# Research protocol

Research precedes teaching authorship. Each assigned researcher performs a broad search, a written reflection, and focused follow-up searches. The integrator closes the research gate only after source support spans all 35 domains and the selected teaching resources have bounded substantive inspections.

The SWE index is a discovery aid. Search both literature nodes and concept-layer works, consult original records for conflicting editions, and treat its verification flags as historical bibliographic evidence. The Knowledge library is the actual document source. Pin its current pointers and manifests; query the catalog read-only and verify rebased payload locations. Do not modify the library, census, sidecars or index. Exclude retained nonactive material and opaque packages.

For each candidate, distinguish identity verification, title/contents inspection and substantive passage inspection. Record exact inspected locators, versions, date, method, limits and reasons for selection or rejection. Failed searches do not establish absence. Historical papers explain mechanisms; current standards and implementation documents establish present contracts. A public draft is not a published ISO standard.

Public contribution records carry titles, identities, URLs, bounded locators and original summaries. Source extracts, paths, watermarks and personal provenance remain in research/private and are excluded from the handbook and release. Do not transmit private paths or extracts to web searches. No source-reading claim may exceed its actual inspection scope.

The research synthesis must identify missing prerequisites, weak areas, contradictory authority, obsolete assumptions, reading overload and platform uncertainty. Focused searches address these findings. An unresolved essential teaching claim blocks its affected module; nonblocking access and experiment limits remain visible.

After this gate, authors select readings only within verified locator scopes. New reading assignments require additional passage inspection. Implementation briefs remain original work; they do not substitute library algorithms for mechanisms the learner must implement.

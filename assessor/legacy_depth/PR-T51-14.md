## T51.14.a — Derive and defend the state, ownership and ordering contract for object teardown and owner-death recovery.

Close changes service policy; destroy requires quiescence of waiters, owners, callbacks and references. A generation check prevents a stale logical handle from reaching new storage but does not make dereferencing an already-freed pointer safe.

Reject: Close and destroy are the same operation.

Assessor discriminator: require the first state, result, ownership edge or schedule event that differs under the rejected explanation. A generic stress checksum or missing sanitizer finding is not a substitute for that witness. Confirm the claim stays inside the declared execution and source scope.

Delayed assessor check: Add a timer holding an implicit reference and derive the cancellation/join step before reclamation.

## T51.14.b — Implement close/drain/destroy states for one queue and an owner-death result for one mutex. Use generation handles and a supervisor that repairs a deliberately half-updated record.

Exit refusal prevents one route to abandonment; it does not handle unexpected death. Recovery must distinguish inconsistent ownership from ordinary unlock, assign exactly one repair owner and preserve an unrecoverable terminal state when repair fails.

Reject: Refusing cooperative owner exit implements recovery from owner death.

Assessor discriminator: require the first state, result, ownership edge or schedule event that differs under the rejected explanation. A generic stress checksum or missing sanitizer finding is not a substitute for that witness. Confirm the claim stays inside the declared execution and source scope.

A generic stress checksum or missing sanitizer finding is not a substitute for that witness. Confirm the claim stays inside the declared execution and source scope.

## T53.05.a — Derive and defend the state, ownership and ordering contract for smp spinlocks and interrupt context.

Modulo progression can work while each live ticket remains unique; admitting a full modulus of outstanding tickets can alias ownership. MCS nodes remain live through queue linkage and release, including interrupt/preemption effects. FIFO ticket admission does not guarantee CPU scheduling fairness.

Reject: A wrapping ticket counter is always safe regardless of outstanding acquisitions.

Assessor discriminator: require the first state, result, ownership edge or schedule event that differs under the rejected explanation. A generic stress checksum or missing sanitizer finding is not a substitute for that witness. Confirm the claim stays inside the declared execution and source scope.

Delayed assessor check: Use one MCS node per CPU and analyze nested interrupt acquisition that reuses the same node.

## T53.05.b — Implement test-and-set and ticket locks, then compare with an MCS queue-lock model. Add IRQ-save wrappers that restore the previous local mask and audit lock ordering.

Masking controls only the local CPU and restoration must preserve preexisting mask state. Inter-CPU exclusion needs the shared atomic/lock protocol. ISR and task acquisition paths must share a declared lock order and context policy.

Reject: Local IRQ masking is an SMP lock, or unlock may always enable interrupts.

Assessor discriminator: require the first state, result, ownership edge or schedule event that differs under the rejected explanation. A generic stress checksum or missing sanitizer finding is not a substitute for that witness. Confirm the claim stays inside the declared execution and source scope.

Delayed assessor check: Nest two IRQ-save protected locks and derive the saved-state and global lock-order requirements.

Waiting location, coherence traffic, preemption and critical-section size affect results. A queue lock may stall behind a descheduled owner. Finite stress and QEMU timings cannot prove fairness or physical scalability; preserve semantic equivalence before comparing costs.

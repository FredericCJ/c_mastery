# Assessor analysis: PR-T64-05

The mathematical norm may be representable even when naive squared components overflow. Scaling changes intermediate magnitudes while preserving the desired mathematical result.

State the scale/sumsq invariant in real arithmetic and discuss floating error separately. Prove why the ratio is bounded and identify final overflow as distinct from avoidable intermediate overflow.

The reference implementation is in `core.c`; inspect it together with the public test. The named fault is: The naive mutant squares3e200 before scaling and rejects a representable5e200 result. Testing only unit-scale data fails to distinguish the two algorithms.

Require the learner to derive 25 from the fixture inputs independently. Maintain norm squared as scale squared times sumsq, but never materialize scale squared.

Transfer answer obligations: Handle subnormal inputs under gradual underflow and a documented flush-to-zero target. Compare results and generated instructions, then revise the admitted accuracy contract.

Required extension evidence includes scalar versus explicit-vector differential tests, emitted assembly and target feature checks, fast-math counterexamples, reproducible resource/timing experiments and changed-size/data-layout transfer. Portable core tests alone do not establish SIMD execution or a performance advantage.

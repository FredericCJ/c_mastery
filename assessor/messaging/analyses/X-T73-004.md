# X-T73-004 assessor technical analysis

The generation is part of the protocol address, not a native pointer validity proof. One executor serializes mailbox and handler state. Death ends that incarnation and clears copy-only messages; ownership-bearing messages require destructors. A channel adapter may safely enqueue on the actor executor but direct foreign-thread enqueue needs synchronization.

## Delayed case X-T73-004-DELAYED

Messages become owned buffers and handlers may enqueue to themselves: implement deterministic destruction of abandoned messages without recursive dispatch. Delayed assessment receives a late reply addressed to a previous incarnation after restart; route it to an explicit dead-letter sink and prevent delivery to the replacement actor.

Require the revised contract, minimal counterexample, implementation/trace and cost evidence before feedback. Assess the invariant under the new assumptions, not just output similarity. The hidden reply retains generation1 while the replacement uses generation2 at the same storage address. A pointer-only check incorrectly accepts it. ## Implemented owning mailbox and dead-letter successor

The actual MsgActor serializes handler calls. Its wrapper removes the current
record into a local owner and passes only a borrowed descriptor to the handler.
Self-send enqueues a distinct owner without recursive dispatch. The wrapper drops
current ownership after the handler returns, then, on failure, drops every queued
owner before the base actor erases its queue. The finite test includes a message
self-enqueued by the failing handler; omitting it leaks despite clearing queue
length. Destroy/restart while stepping cannot be used as an escape from this rule.

An old incarnation's reply is consumed by the explicit dead-letter callback and
is never delivered to the new actor. FULL/CLOSED retain caller ownership while
STALE consumes into the sink: these deliberately different rules must be stated
at the interface. A generation token is not authentication. The delayed test
restarts, submits the old generation, observes one sink transfer/destruction and
zero new-actor delivery, then checks current-generation self-enqueue and finite
retirement. The controls omit queued-owner cleanup or erase the generation check.
No Erlang process isolation, thread-safe actor calls or asynchronous exception
unwind follows from this C wrapper.

This analysis records the technical solution and expected reasoning, not learner performance or a non-author review. Public-source comparisons are restricted to the registered inspected passages. Tests, sanitizers and bounded models do not prove arbitrary lifetimes, interleavings or environments correct.
## Executable channel-to-actor boundary

The required adapter is `followthrough/actor_executor.c` against the public
`ACTOR_EXECUTOR.md` contract. The channel accepts a unique envelope carrying the
submitted incarnation. Its receiver extracts local ownership, releases the
envelope, and only then dispatches the actual actor. An accepted sender must
not inspect that envelope after publication: a different thread may already
have delivered its payload and reused the slot. Failed publication leaves the
payload owner with the caller, while failed **actor** admission after channel
acceptance transfers it to the dead-letter sink. These are different boundaries.

One executor thread owns actor state. Handler self-enqueue is a nonblocking
channel send, not a recursive call to pump; blocking on one's own full input
would prevent the only executor from draining it. A restart increments the
published identity but never rewrites queued envelopes. The generation mutant
demonstrates the otherwise plausible error of delivering an old reply to a new
actor. The envelope-release mutant exhausts fixed metadata even though payload
destructors still run. Both are semantic failures, not sanitizer surrogates.

The tests also preserve a synthetic effect emitted before handler failure,
exercise accepted/unaccepted rendezvous, and retain active calls during BUSY
destruction. The independent state-machine oracle checks counters and cleanup
for finite send/pump/restart/close/cancel words. The2,000-message native trace
compares a separate mutex-protected state under the observed order. It does not
prove arbitrary scheduler histories or benchmark queueing against an unrelated
mutex workload. This is bounded author support for R2/R3/R4 and the original
late-reply delayed variant, not an independent learner assessment.

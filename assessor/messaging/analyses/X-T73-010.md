# X-T73-010 assessor technical analysis

Request updates credit but never emits synchronously in this subset, so callback requests cannot create unbounded recursion. Emit marks delivering, consumes credit and retains storage until return. Terminal state precedes its callback and is permanent. A multithreaded adapter needs both serialization and retained lifetime; making demand atomic alone solves neither.

## Assessment boundaries

The assessor implementation is messaging.c in this separate directory tree. Review D1–D4 individually against the metadata rubric. The deliberate SC_MUTANT values 10 and 20 targets the streams anchor; require its named semantic assertion, not a compiler failure, timeout or unrelated crash. Additional independent tests remain mandatory.

## Delayed case X-T73-010-DELAYED

Make signals originate on several threads: serialize them through one executor and retain the subscription until all in-flight signals quiesce. Delayed assessment introduces a slow subscriber and bounded channel capacity; compare blocking, dropping and failure policies under the same stated delivery contract, with T75 owning the overload decision.

Require the revised contract, minimal counterexample, implementation/trace and cost evidence before feedback. Assess the invariant under the new assumptions, not just output similarity. Pause one next callback, cancel from another source and enqueue terminal completion. A valid executor serializes the signals and retains the context through the paused callback; freeing on cancel is a lifetime violation. The overload policy must name which accepted items may be discarded and how that affects demand accounting.

## Limits

This analysis records the technical solution and expected reasoning, not learner performance or a non-author review. Public-source comparisons are restricted to the registered inspected passages. Tests, sanitizers and bounded models do not prove arbitrary lifetimes, interleavings or environments correct.

## Reentrant invalid demand repair

A request0 inside next originally returned BUSY and lost the required error. The repaired protocol retains a pending error until next returns, then emits terminal INVALID without nested signals. Cancellation by that callback suppresses the pending notification under the explicit local policy. Tests cover both transitions. This policy is part of the original subset, not a claim of JVM certification.

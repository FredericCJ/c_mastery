Request updates credit but never emits synchronously in this subset, so callback requests cannot create unbounded recursion. Emit marks delivering, consumes credit and retains storage until return. Terminal state precedes its callback and is permanent. A multithreaded adapter needs both serialization and retained lifetime; making demand atomic alone solves neither.

## Delayed case X-T73-010-DELAYED

Make signals originate on several threads: serialize them through one executor and retain the subscription until all in-flight signals quiesce. Delayed assessment introduces a slow subscriber and bounded channel capacity; compare blocking, dropping and failure policies under the same stated delivery contract, with T75 owning the overload decision.

Require the revised contract, minimal counterexample, implementation/trace and cost evidence before feedback. Assess the invariant under the new assumptions, not just output similarity. Pause one next callback, cancel from another source and enqueue terminal completion. The overload policy must name which accepted items may be discarded and how that affects demand accounting.

## Executable executor and overload successor

`followthrough/stream.c` composes the actual copied-value hub and demand core.
Only the executor reads or writes the core; producer calls publish pending
controls or items under the control mutex, with control-before-hub lock order.
Callbacks execute after releasing locks, so callback requests enqueue work
without nested next invocation. Idle acknowledges only currently processable
work; queued zero-demand items may remain. Its condition predicate is necessary
to reject invented-credit control81 deterministically, without timing sleeps.

Lease count protects subscription/context lifetime beyond cancellation and even
beyond worker join. Destroy must reject outstanding handles (control82) before
freeing context or mutexes. Producer admission closes before pending cancellation
or terminal processing; an already-running callback still returns normally.
Cancellation has priority over an unstarted terminal. Exactly one worker then
discards all remaining accepted items and finishes. Only an actual join plus
released handles and administrative quiescence permits destruction. A handle is
an affine convention, not an unforgeable or compiler-enforced capability.

Pause callback0, fill capacity2, and distinguish the three admission policies
without changing the delivery contract. WAIT must enroll and later admit item3;
DROP rejects item3 while delivering the accepted three; FAIL rejects item3,
finishes callback0 and discards the other accepted two. Account for in-flight
items separately until callback return. Controls83/84 reject wrong wait-resume
and missing failure-terminal behavior. Cancellation wakes blocked producers
before the paused callback returns; this does not authorize context release.
Callback WAIT on a full queue returns BUSY to prevent self-deadlock.

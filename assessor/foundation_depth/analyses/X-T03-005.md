# X-T03-005 assessor analysis

Assessor-only finite identity analysis. Do not demonstrate dangling-owner behavior by dereferencing or comparing an expired native pointer.

## Technical solution

Initialization sets each entry generation1/live0/retired0. Acquire scans in increasing index order, copying an FDValue into the first nonlive/nonretired entry and publishing {owner,index,generation}. Admission precedes output writes. Get/release first check owner identity and index, then live and generation. Wrong owner, index, old generation or duplicate release are stale.

Release generation1 or2 makes the slot free and increments generation; release generation3 retires it permanently. The invariant prohibits retired entries being live/reallocated. A released handle from a permitted pool lifetime can never match a later live generation of that slot. At most 3*3=9 acquisitions can succeed with suitable releases; retirement is capacity consumed by finite identity history.

The owner itself must remain a stable live object. Copying/reinitializing a pool while old handles survive breaks the contract; an owner-pointer field cannot validate its own lifetime. Calling lookup with a destroyed owner is outside the API's admitted behavior, even if the address has been reused. Handles are forgeable logical identifiers, not authentication capabilities.

## Independent histories and rubric

Model a slot as Free(g), Live(g,value) or Retired. Enumerate its complete three-generation transition chain and illegal gets/releases; combine slots with a lowest-index allocation choice. Retain every old handle and check all after each reuse. A wrap mutant revives generation1 after release at3; the actual repair must retire instead.

Require separate validity rules: a copied FDValue remains independent; a handle's usability depends on the owner and current generation; a pointer returned by a variant would borrow the current stored object and become semantically invalid on release. Rust ownership reasoning is not a guarantee of arbitrary logical handle freshness; its documented move rule and this runtime identity protocol solve different obligations.

## Withheld delayed variant: X-T03-005-DELAYED

Propose resetting all three retired slots during maintenance. Supply a retained generation1 handle and show that a naïve reset revalidates it. Require either proof that all handles are discarded before reinitialization under a new owner lifetime, or an external epoch registry. If the registry epoch is finite, force its exhaustion too; increasing bit width delays the counterexample but does not remove it.

Use two live pool owners and a registry whose mappings may move. Correct handles identify stable registry slot/epoch, and lookup validates both registry and local slot identity before returning a copied value. The learner must account for registry lifetime and limits; do not accept a new raw pointer as an unexplained fix. No full registry implementation is supplied.

## Limits

A finite slot test and generation proof establish no concurrency, memory reclamation or unbounded identity theorem. The taxonomy's broader formal provenance-specification comparison is not covered by this handle exercise.
